# de Grey's question, answered -- and why the criterion cannot decide it

de Grey asked publicly (Polymath16 thread 18, 13 Sep 2026) for someone to
explore Haugland's Moser-spindle-free construction as a route past 509, "using
more than just two sets of 42 vectors".  The joining criterion was ported to
`Q(zeta_420)` to answer it.  The answer is that **the criterion passes
automatically here and therefore says nothing** -- and that is a proof, not a
failed computation.

## The machinery, which does work

`hn/cyclolat.py` carries the trace form into cyclotomic fields.  `Q(zeta_n)` is
abelian, so complex conjugation lies in its Galois group and commutes with every
automorphism; hence every conjugate of a unit vector is a unit vector and

    |z| = 1   ==>   Tr(z conj(z)) = phi(n),

a positive definite form, so the unit vectors are the integer points of one
ellipsoid.  On the power basis the Gram matrix is Toeplitz, with `Tr(zeta^k)` the
Ramanujan sum.  Validated: the lattice generated by one set of 42 has exactly 42
unit vectors -- the generators themselves.

Two measurements worth keeping:

* Haugland's lattice has **`Q`-rank 12**, not the 96 its field suggests, and the
  joint lattice with `theta_4` has **rank 24** -- constant in the number of sets.
  So the enumeration is tractable, which was not obvious.
* More sets **do** enrich the lattice: the covolume falls by a factor of 7 each
  time (pivot products `7^11`, `7^10`, `7^9` over `7^12` for 2, 3, 4 sets).
  de Grey's suggestion is not cosmetic.

## Why the criterion is vacuous here

Haugland joins by `theta_4`, whose shell sits at radius **2**.  For any generator
`g` of any lattice closed under its own generators, `2g` is a shell point, and

    (2g - theta_4(2g)) / 2  =  g - theta_4(g),

which lies in the joint lattice by construction, with modulus
`2 sin(theta_4 / 2) = 1/2`.  So a half-unit vector exists **automatically**, the
criterion passes, and it passes for the same reason whatever the lattice is.

Measured and consistent: one set of 42 gives a joint lattice with 210 unit
vectors of which exactly **42** lie in `2 Lambda` -- one per generator, as the
argument predicts.  Running two and three sets would return 84 and 126 by the
same argument, at a cost that grows sharply, and would add nothing.

## What this settles

* **The direction is not dead.**  The criterion cannot kill it, so Haugland's
  construction remains a genuine candidate -- and its joint lattice is *richer*
  than the record's: 210 unit vectors against the Moser lattice's 126.
* **The criterion cannot decide it either.**  Any rotation whose shell radius is
  an even integer passes for free, and `theta_4`'s is 2.  Deciding de Grey's
  question needs either a different rotation -- one whose shell radius is not an
  even integer, which the classification can now supply -- or the SAT route with
  its ball, which is what everyone has been doing.

That last point is the useful one: the classification lists rotations with odd or
irrational shell radii, and those are the ones where the test has content.
Applying it to the heptagonal lattice with such a rotation is the next step, and
it is now cheap.
